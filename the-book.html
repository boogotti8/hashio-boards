How to read it
1 · the Monitor is the book
Price, the weekly arrow and the 200d floor are read live, verbatim — never re-derived. The weekly arrow times the move; it never sets the score.
2 · the Regime is the framework
Verdict reduce risk, season Summer · liquidity rising. The cycle tilts the whole book by -13.57 pts — it moves everything, it overrides nothing.
3 · the score is value + cycle
Two legs — the asset vs its own exponential trend, and vs what US net liquidity implies. ±1σ → ±55, ±2σ → ±85. Right = cheap.
4 · cheap only becomes sell on structure
A long-term trend change needs 2 consecutive weekly closes below the 200d floor — that’s the dashed ring. Cheap without the tape is a value trap, not a buy.

HOW THIS IS BUILT — PUBLIC DATA ONLY
THE MASTER READ (the dot's position) = 100 * tanh(cheap sigma / 1.6) + regime tilt, clamped to +/-100 — so +/-1 sigma = +/-55 and +/-2 sigma = +/-85. cheap sigma = -(1/2 * own-trend leg + 1/2 * US-native liquidity leg), i.e. the sign flip of the valuation composite on the sibling map: BOTH legs are z-scores of the last close against a trailing 4y (~1008 session) regression — leg 1 is log(price) on time (distance from the asset's own exponential trend), leg 2 is log(price) on log(US net liquidity = Fed balance sheet - TGA - reverse repo, FRED WALCL/WTREGEN/RRPONTSYD). Positive = cheap = right. THE REGIME TILT moves the whole book together and never overrides a holding's own value: 1/2 the live Cycle Gauge + 1/4 of Markets' Fuel, capped at +/-15 points. THE FLOOR = the 200-session moving average; a long-term trend change is only CONFIRMED on 2 consecutive weekly closes below it (the dashed ring on a dot). THE WEEKLY ARROW (up/down above each dot, and on every chip) is the weekly MACD(12,26,9) histogram sign — timing only, deliberately kept out of the score: it says when, not what it is worth. THE MONITOR ROW is read verbatim from live price, never re-derived. No valuation is ever invented for a name with less than 504 sessions of history, or for one whose old channel is the wrong yardstick — those ride below the scale as chips and the tape decides.
