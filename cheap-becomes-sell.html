How to read it
1 · the verdict sets the corner
Macro verdict = reduce risk. Defensive: the bottom row leads to de-risk and the rich edge is exit territory — the buy zone only pays while the floor holds.
2 ↕ structure decides if it’s in play
Above the dashed line = long-term uptrend intact (price over the 200d floor and the floor rising). Below = breaking, and cheap stops being an opportunity.
3 ↔ the band is the level
Own-trend leg ↔ US-liquidity leg; the dot is the ½+½ composite. Tight band = both agree (conviction); wide = divergence is the signal. Faint halo = anchor sensitivity (±2y).
4 ▲/▼ the weekly arrow is the timing
Red in the buy zone = the dip; green at the rich edge = don’t chase. Kept out of the score deliberately — it times the level, it doesn’t set it.

HOW THIS IS BUILT — PUBLIC DATA ONLY
VALUATION composite sigma (sign: + = RICH / expensive, drawn on the LEFT; - = cheap, drawn right) — two legs, each a z-score of the last close against a trailing 4y (~1008 session) regression. LEG 1 own-trend: log(price) regressed on time — how far price sits from its own exponential trend. LEG 2 US-native liquidity: log(price) regressed on log(US net liquidity = Fed balance sheet - TGA - reverse repo, FRED WALCL/WTREGEN/RRPONTSYD) — how rich or cheap it is versus what liquidity alone implies. The dot is the 1/2+1/2 mean; the horizontal band spans the two legs (tight = both agree, wide = divergence is the signal); the faint halo is the same read re-run on 2y and 4y windows (anchor sensitivity). LONG-TERM TREND — z-score of log(price/200d MA) averaged with the z-score of the 200d MA's 60-session slope: above 0 and above the line = structure intact. THE FLOOR = the 200d MA; a long-term trend change is only CONFIRMED on 2 consecutive weekly closes below it — an intraweek pierce that closes back above is a spike low, not a trend change. THE WEEKLY ARROW is the weekly MACD(12,26,9) histogram sign: timing only, deliberately kept out of the score.
